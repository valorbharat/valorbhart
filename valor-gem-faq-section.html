GeM (Government e-Marketplace) is India's official online procurement platform where central and state government departments, PSUs, and autonomous bodies buy goods and services directly from registered sellers. Any manufacturer, MSME, trader, service provider, or corporate entity with a valid business registration and GST (where applicable) can register as a seller — you don't need to be a large company or have prior government dealings to get started.

At a minimum you'll need PAN, GSTIN, business registration proof (Udyam/MSME certificate, incorporation certificate, or partnership deed depending on entity type), a cancelled cheque or bank proof, Aadhaar of the authorised signatory, and a valid Digital Signature Certificate (DSC) Class 3. Additional documents like ISO/BIS certification, past turnover proof, or product test reports may be needed depending on the category you want to list under.

GeM does not charge a registration fee — the portal itself is free to join. However, sellers do need a paid Class 3 DSC and, in most cases, a nominal caution money deposit depending on their category and turnover. Registration itself can be completed in a day if your documents are in order, but getting your profile fully optimised — correct category mapping, brand approval, catalog listing — usually takes longer if done without guidance, which is where most delays actually happen.

A Bid is a sealed, one-time quotation process — you submit your best technical and financial offer once, and the buyer evaluates all sellers' submissions after the bid closes. A Reverse Auction (RA) is a live, time-bound bidding event where pre-qualified sellers compete by progressively lowering their price in real time, and the lowest bidder at closing wins. Bids reward strong upfront preparation; RAs reward speed and pricing discipline during the live window.

Each bid document lays out specific eligibility conditions — minimum average annual turnover over the last 3 years, past experience value (often as a percentage of the estimated bid value), required certifications (ISO, BIS, OEM authorisation), and sometimes MSE/Startup relaxations. Missing even one condition, or submitting documents that don't exactly match the format asked for, is the single biggest reason bids get technically rejected — which is why careful eligibility mapping before submission matters more than the price you quote.

Registered Micro and Small Enterprises (MSEs) and DPIIT-recognised Startups are eligible for relaxations such as EMD (Earnest Money Deposit) exemption, prior turnover and experience relaxation on many bids, and priority under government procurement policy targets that reserve a portion of annual procurement for MSEs. These benefits only apply automatically if your Udyam/Startup status is correctly linked and verified on your GeM seller profile.

GeM evaluates technical eligibility before it ever looks at price. Common rejection reasons include incomplete or expired certificates, turnover figures that don't match your GST filings, missing OEM authorisation letters, wrong product specification mapping, or documents uploaded in the wrong format. A technically non-responsive bid is disqualified regardless of how low the price is — this is exactly the stage where most self-filed bids fail.

Once an order is confirmed, the seller delivers as per the agreed timeline and uploads delivery/installation proof. Payment is released by the buyer department through the Consignee Receipt and Acceptance Certificate (CRAC) process, typically within a defined payment cycle after acceptance — GeM's structure is designed to reduce the delayed-payment problems common in traditional government tendering.

GeM's rules, eligibility formats, and category-specific requirements change frequently, and a single missed clause is enough to get a technically strong bid rejected.
